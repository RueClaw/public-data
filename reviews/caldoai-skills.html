Change-together reasoning

The strongest idea is to ask whether pieces should change together. Hidden links cover duplicated logic and implicit order; needless links cover leaked internals and volatile dependencies; surplus covers pass-through wrappers, unused extension points, and dead code. This organizes design review, but is not a machine-checkable definition of correctness. Lines 30–68.

A decision log connecting causes to fixes

Explicit opt-in for comments and test pruning

The agent must ask whether to remove redundant comments and prune or rewrite weak tests; those additions are optional. Test cleanup is deferred until after structural work, acknowledging the regression safety net. Distinguishing implementation-coupled tests from behavioral tests is useful, but whether a test catches a real bug remains a judgment call. A mistaken deletion can conceal a regression. Consent is partial. Step 2 says not to change anything while cataloging problems. After redesign, the next step is “Fix everything on the list”; there is no mandatory user approval of that list. Stopping and correctness are underspecified. “Do not stop until there is nothing worth fixing left” relies on subagent judgment; there is no iteration, token, cost, or time cap. Behavior must be preserved except bugs fixed along the way, with testing at the end. No pre-change test baseline, per-change regression check, branch/checkpoint, rollback, dirty-worktree protection, or failure escalation is required. Incidental bug fixes also complicate a behavior-preserving comparison. Lines 88–107.

Heuristics need domain constraints. Necessary special cases may remain, but counting them is called the best measure of progress. Retries, sleeps, provider exceptions, or unusual comments may encode real distributed-system or business constraints. Removing them needs operational evidence, not just cleaner dependencies. The tip to “script away the function bodies” does not specify a disposable copy or read-only representation; treat that as an analysis transformation, never erasure of working sources. Lines 56–74, 94–107.

No outbound automation is prescribed. There are no instructions to push commits, open PRs, send messages, deploy, or contact a Caldo API. There is also no embedded protection against untrusted instructions encountered in the target repository. The host agent must preserve its own trust boundaries and approval controls.
