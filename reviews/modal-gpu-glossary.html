Key points

What matters most, in order.

1

Strong cross-layer map

The introduction and five sampled entries connect hardware, threads, and compiler targets. The Ada/Ampere distinction and Hopper/Blackwell target exceptions are useful details.

2

Do not turn analogies into contracts

Resident warp selection is not GPU process preemption, and shared memory is not the hardware-managed L1 interface. Volta and later require care with assumptions of warp lock-step.

3

Small syntax error, clear scope limit

The CUDA C++ entry says shared rather than __shared__, and describes kernel arguments too narrowly. This is a NVIDIA-focused reference, not an exhaustive GPU course or a hardware benchmark.

Useful distinctions

The architecture and compute-capability entries link virtual PTX targets to physical SM targets without equating marketing names with major version numbers. Architecture-specific a-targets and family-specific f-targets require more care than a SemVer analogy suggests.

Corrections to carry forward

Use __shared__ for shared-memory declarations. Kernels may accept scalar arguments as well as pointers. Do not assume implicit warp synchronization or a permanent one-thread-to-one-core mapping.
