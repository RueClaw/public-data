Key points

What matters most, in order.

1

Small download, much larger working set

Standard is advertised at 163 MB. The MLX loader estimates 6 GB during unpacking; the card reports about 15 GB peak for a ten-minute recording. These are publisher figures, not reproduced here.

2

The default is Clean, not the headline model

Clean removes fillers and false starts and reports 5.83% mean WER on verbatim benchmarks; Standard reports 4.94%. Cleanup is useful for dictation, not automatically suitable for exact quotations.

3

Local transcription is not zero metadata collection

The Mac privacy policy discloses accounts, optional usage counts and ordinary daily check-ins; the website uses Meta measurement. App security claims and native binary behavior were not audited.

Claims versus evidence

The standard card reports 4.937% mean WER versus Parakeet’s 4.941%: a tiny 0.004-point margin, while AMI shows a larger reported improvement. Bark rows are publisher results, explicitly not submitted leaderboard entries. No confidence intervals or complete evaluation artifacts were found in the inspected inventory.

Implementation is inspectable

The loader decodes entropy-coded tensors into floating-point Parakeet weights. MLX uses a cached safetensors conversion; PyTorch fetches upstream processor/configuration on first use. Optional re-quantization must be evaluated separately.

Missing capabilities

English only, no word timings or individual speaker names, and no Bark 3.0 custom-word-list support on the product page. Standard standalone iOS Core ML guidance says not yet tested on iPhone/iPad.

Trust boundaries

Model headers and compressed blobs are trusted inputs without explicit resource bounds. Standalone loaders do not visibly pin model checksums; a wrapper imports the chosen Python loader as executable code. Pin revisions and verify files. No exploitation or network audit was performed.

App versus model

Local audio processing does not establish absence of account telemetry, provider connections or website tracking. Accessibility authority and meeting consent need separate consideration. Published security assessments remain vendor statements; named penetration tests are still underway.

Free, with access and license caveats

The pricing page’s $1,999 plan is a joke explicitly replaced by “100% free.” No paid transcription quota is disclosed. Mac access is invite-only; the app has proprietary personal-use terms. Model CC-BY-4.0 permits reuse with attribution and change notices, not redistribution of the proprietary app.

Verdict

The call, and why.
