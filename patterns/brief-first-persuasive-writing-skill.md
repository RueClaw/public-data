## Pattern

Make persuasive-writing agents establish the brief before drafting. The agent should identify the reader, desired response, evidence, objections, channel, constraints, and speaker voice, then draft only after those inputs are known or explicitly assumed.

## Workflow

1. Gather the brief.
   - Desired reader belief, decision, feeling, or action.
   - Audience identity, incentives, prior beliefs, and likely concerns.
   - Required proof, examples, facts, or offer details.
   - Objections that could block the desired response.
   - Channel, length, tone, deadline, and voice constraints.

2. Map the argument.
   - Central claim or reader benefit.
   - One to three strongest supporting reasons.
   - Objection handling when it improves trust.
   - Specific next step.

3. Draft in reader order.
   - Open with relevance or consequence.
   - Put the main point before background.
   - Keep each paragraph to one stage of the argument.
   - Match formality to channel and audience.

4. Prune for clarity.
   - Remove filler and repeated claims.
   - Prefer concrete words and active constructions.
   - Keep sentences short enough to follow.
   - Preserve uncertainty that affects the decision.

5. Remove generated-prose tells.
   - Check for formulaic contrast structures.
   - Remove canned openings and transitions.
   - Reduce stock AI vocabulary.
   - Break mechanical rhythm and repeated rule-of-three phrasing.
   - Replace abstract labels with concrete nouns and verbs.
   - Avoid decorative emphasis that does not help the reader decide.

6. Final check.
   - The point is clear on first pass.
   - Claims are supported.
   - The likely objection is handled or intentionally omitted.
   - The requested action is concrete and easy to take.
   - The speaker still sounds like a specific person.

## Why It Matters

Most generated persuasive copy fails before drafting starts. It guesses the audience, invents support, avoids the real objection, or produces a polished paragraph with no clear action. A brief-first skill turns the agent into a writing partner that asks for the missing business context before optimizing sentences.

## Adoption Notes

This pattern is especially useful for emails, proposals, fundraising asks, sales notes, announcements, policy arguments, and stakeholder memos. It should be interruptible: if the user explicitly asks the agent to make assumptions, the agent can proceed while stating the material assumptions.

For shared use, pair the skill with examples and a small before/after evaluation set. The evaluation should check whether the draft preserves facts, makes the ask clear, handles objections fairly, avoids unsupported pressure, and sounds like the requested speaker.
