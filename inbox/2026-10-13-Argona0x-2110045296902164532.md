## Article text

most people use AI like a stranger. every new chat starts at zero, and you explain your work, your people and your voice all over again.
a second brain ends that. it is one folder that knows everything you know, and an AI that reads it before every task. unlike a chat, it gets sharper every week you feed it.
Andrej Karpathy described it in April as a knowledge base that an AI writes and keeps for you, and his post drew 22 million views. one post sharing a build of it has 85,000 bookmarks.
but the popular builds live on a laptop. the most saved guide tells you to "leave Obsidian open, the connection only works while the app is running."
a brain that does the work needs a computer that never closes.
Grok Bot gives it one. its AI helpers, called bots, work on a computer of their own in the cloud, and that computer keeps going while your laptop is shut.
Kevin Rose already built one there: a bot that indexes every Instagram video he saved into a Karpathy style wiki.
 
this is the 7-step course that builds yours. it files what comes in overnight, hands you one page every morning, and every Sunday it tells you what you decided and forgot.
keep the brain in files, not in the chat. a bot's memory keeps short summaries, while a file on its computer stays exactly as written, and every bot you add later can read it.
tonight you need the app and twenty emails you sent. chapter 01 turns them into a file about you.

01. a file about you
every email you sent already says who you work with, what you promised and how you sound.
Kevin Niparko, a product manager at SpaceXAI, the company behind Grok Bot, ranks one instruction above every other he has tried: "read my past Slack messages and develop a Slack style guide for me."
 
Grok Bot runs on Mac, Windows, Linux, iPhone and Android, from x.ai/bot. it signs in with a Cursor account and comes with Cursor's paid plans from $20 a month, or with SuperGrok or X Premium+ linked to that account. the free trial lasts up to 7 days.
open the desktop app and choose Sign in.

an older account uses + at the top of the sidebar and Create new Bot. pick Notes, docs and tasks when it asks what to help with, then right-click it, choose Rename Bot and call it Brain.

02. the folder
a bot's memory keeps your preferences and short summaries of past work. SpaceXAI's docs say memory is not a substitute for the source, the app where a fact lives.
files on the bot's computer are visible to every bot, and the docs say to keep lasting files in `/workspace`. so the brain splits facts in two:
slow facts, like who people are and how you work, live in files.
fast facts, like status, dates and amounts, are reopened at the source on every run.
 
the description holds the rules a bot keeps in every chat. Brain shows it back with the folder tree.
 
what lasts sits in files, and what changes is checked at the source.
