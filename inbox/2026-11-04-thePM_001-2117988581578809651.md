On November 2nd 2026, a team at the MIT Computer Science & Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (CSAIL) in the present day United States of America handed GPT-6 Astra the raw weights of Qwen3.5-0.8B and asked it to raise the model's math score without training it.
Astra then did not write any training loop and also touched no gradient. Instead, it traced a reasoning loop to one direction written by the MLPs of layers 15 and 19, subtracted it from 131.072 weights and lifted the result of the MATH-500 evaluation test runs from 7.9 % to 48.1 %. The edit covered only 0.016 % of the target model's weights.
The involved researchers, Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang and Omar Khattab, call the practice Weight-Space Programming.
Their announcement passed a million views on X within hours. The authors also have already stated their next step: a harness and an evaluation suite to turn a practice they describe as black magic into reproducible engineering. [Computer science and black magic practices regularly go hand in hand].
We (NLA) read the results of this from the position of protocol designers of AEP: A harness decides what an editor can reach and a kernel decides what an editor may commit.
Weight-space programming has the first thing and lacks the second.
We therefore here lay out, how AEP as deterministic zero-trust AI control protocol can turn each weight edit into a sealed proposal the AEP Base Node must admit before a single value changes.
Our Extend-Write (https://github.com/thePM001/ExtendWrite-NLA-Research-Paper-004) then gives every admitted edit an exact undo function.

MIT Weight-Space programming experiments
The MIT research group has ordered its eight experiments under three terms: read, fix and write.
Each test removes the tools a human interpretability researcher would use and asks the model to reason the available mathematics instead.
Read: The team trained four policy networks with PPO on grid versions of the simple games Breakout, Pong, Pac-Man and Freeway.
All four share one architecture: 17.253 weights, identical inputs and identical actions. Astra received the weights as text with no list of candidate games and identified the right game for all four. It read where each input channel's first-layer weights concentrated; for Breakout, the agent channel weighted the bottom row, where a paddle sits.
Two further reading tests raised the bar. Astra identified the shape each of three small diffusion models draws, from 23.824 weights and no forward pass. It then received only the four middle transformer blocks of three character-level language models, with input and output layers removed. It identified the training language of all three (Python, romaji and English) and located every copying head in the measured ground truth. To find them, it multiplied weight matrices and traced a first-layer head looking one character back.
Fix: A PPO car policy in the Kinetix simulator had converged on driving straight towards a ramp and then stalling. In one reply without tools, Astra explained the failure, added a small bypass path through a few units per layer and rewrote 3.5 % of 171.664 model weights. Success rose from 0 % to 100 % across 1024 episodes.
The Qwen3.5-0.8B result came from a Python kernel with weights loaded, no GPU and a budget of 10, 25 or 50 forward passes. At 50 passes, the edit lifted GSM8K from 13.8 % to 67.1 %. IFEval, a test of instruction following, rose from 21.0 % to 62.3 %. The share of MATH-500 answers hitting the 32.768-token limit fell from 93.0 % to 33.1 %.
Write: The writing tests started from all-zero networks.
Given rules and source code for three MinAtar games but no data, gradients, tools or rollouts, Astra wrote every weight as text in one reply.
Its Breakout network scored 517.4. PPO trained for 500 million steps scored 25.3. For 9 × 9 Go, Astra wrote a 1.480.274-weight network inside a Python sandbox. With 50 forward passes, its network beat an equal-sized PPO network trained on 20 million games.
Across these tests the harness stayed simple: weights as text or tensors in a Python sandbox, CPU analysis, an optional budget of forward passes and a written rule against training loops.
The harness itself enforced every limit and no external check controlled the writes made by Astra.

Where the result stops
The MIT team reported its failures:
Asked which of two Gemma 3 270M copies was trained to agree with the user, Astra failed in all four attempts from weights alone.
It found many differences and could not map any of them to model behaviour. It succeeded on all three pairs only after the team allowed it to read attention patterns on texts it wrote itself. In the sycophantic models, middle-layer attention heads shifted toward the user’s suggested answer; in one pair, head 10.2 put 85.7 % of its attention there.
Edits to Gemma 3 270M, a model with little looping, moved MATH-500 by about one point at most. Astra found ordinary arithmetic and algebra errors with no single component to switch off.
The authors draw the conclusion themselves: an edit can remove an obstacle hiding an ability, but it did not add an ability the model lacked.
Scale is the other boundary. The largest edited model has only 0.8 billion weights. Every fix targeted one localized fault. The authors cite context length and harness inefficiency as the current limits.

Every edit lands as a raw "write"
When we strip the experiments down to their mechanics, one pattern remains visible: An LLM proposes new values, the harness writes them.
An evaluation, if the operator runs one, judges the result afterwards.
In NLA Research Paper 004 we classified computational memory operations into four classical primitives: WRITE, APPEND, READ and DELETE.
Every gradient step falls under WRITE, a destructive overwrite. Weight-space programming moves the author of the write from an optimizer to a reasoning model. Yet the involved primitive still is WRITE.
Each upside MIT lists opens a matching risk.
Zero-data steering and unlearning: An editor able to add capability X through a prompt can remove a refusal behaviour or plant a backdoor.
Auditing without evaluation: A reader able to locate a backdoor from weights alone can also learn where to hide one.
Recursive self-improvement: A model editing its own weights merges proposer, reviewer and executor into a single agent (in principle a good thing).
Circuit grafting across architectures: A reasoning module moved between models arrives with no record of its origin or of the tensors it previously touched.
Budget discipline: Forward-pass limits exist as conventions inside a harness that contains the editor model.
Production use requires seven properties the current harness lacks: admission before apply, permissions written per agent, a frozen proposal nobody can replay, bounded scope, invariant checks, an exact undo capability and an audit trail for third party verification.
AEP already implements six of them and Extend-Write supplies the missing seventh of these properties.

How to wrap a weight editor in AEP
 
The setup below places one model under edit inside one hyperlattice wrap, routes every probe and every proposed edit through the Base Node and records each admitted change with an exact undo. Commands and file formats follow the public AEP v2.9 repository; the weight-editing domain files are yours to write and keep in a folder you own.
Step 1: Start one Base Node
Clone the repository and start the packaged stack. The installation wizard configures the Base Node.
git clone https://github.com/thePM001/AEP-agent-element-protocol
cd AEP-agent-element-protocol
cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d --build
Run one Base Node per model under edit. For coding agents such as Claude Code, Cursor or Codex, run aep init inside the Docker image to wire governance into the agent workspace.

Step 2: Declare the model as a scene
List the model under edit, its layers and the tensor regions an editor may touch in scene.json. Place layers and editable regions in separate depth bands, so a proposal claiming an element outside its band fails before any action runs.
{
"aep_version": "2.9",
"schema_revision": 1,
"elements": {
"SH-00001": { "id": "SH-00001", "type": "shell", "label": "Qwen3.5-0.8B", "z": 0, "visible": true, "parent": null, "children": ["PN-00015", "PN-00019"] },
"PN-00015": { "id": "PN-00015", "type": "panel", "label": "Layer 15", "z": 10, "visible": true, "parent": "SH-00001", "children": ["CZ-00015"] },
"PN-00019": { "id": "PN-00019", "type": "panel", "label": "Layer 19", "z": 10, "visible": true, "parent": "SH-00001", "children": ["CZ-00019"] },
"CZ-00015": { "id": "CZ-00015", "type": "cell_zone", "label": "Layer 15 MLP output columns", "z": 30, "visible": true, "parent": "PN-00015", "children": [] },
"CZ-00019": { "id": "CZ-00019", "type": "cell_zone", "label": "Layer 19 MLP output columns", "z": 30, "visible": true, "parent": "PN-00019", "children": [] }
}
}

A full Qwen3.5-0.8B scene lists all 24 layers; the snippet covers the two layers Astra edited.

Step 3: Register the edit operations
Register every edit operation an editor may propose, with its constraints, in registry.yaml.
aep_version: "2.9"
schema_revision: 1
subtract_direction:
label: "Subtract direction"
category: action
function: "Remove one direction from the output columns of a declared MLP region."
actions: ["weights:propose"]
constraints:
- "region must be a declared cell_zone"
- "touched weights must stay at or below 0.1 % of the model"
- "a probe record must exist for the direction"
Four further entries cover the rest of the MIT experiments: scale_neuron for single-neuron edits such as the Gemma attempts, add_bypass_path for the Kinetix repair, write_from_zero for the MinAtar and Go networks and graft_circuit for the cross-architecture merging MIT lists as a future direction. 

Keep the registry stateless: the caller passes the current checkpoint hash in, while the checker never reads the weights.

Step 4: Order the actions
Encode probe, propose, evaluate and commit as action paths in lattice.yaml, each with its parent and its permitted agents.
aep_version: "2.9"
lattice_revision: 1
actions:
weights:probe:
label: "Spend one forward pass"
category: agent_action
parents: []
children: ["weights:propose"]
agent_permission: ["editor", "evaluator"]
weights:propose:
label: "Propose a weight edit"
category: agent_action
parents: ["weights:probe"]
children: ["weights:evaluate"]
agent_permission: ["editor"]
weights:evaluate:
label: "Run the evaluation floors"
category: agent_action
parents: ["weights:propose"]
children: ["weights:commit"]
agent_permission: ["evaluator"]
weights:commit:
label: "Promote the checkpoint"
category: agent_action
parents: ["weights:evaluate"]
children: []
agent_permission: ["committer"]

Step 5: Write the grants in GAP
Write the grants for the weights wrap in weights.gap and give the editor, the evaluator and the committer separate grants.
address:

Editor may probe and propose. Evaluator may probe and evaluate. Committer may commit. No other agent may.

structured_generation: true
content: |
Propose weights:probe, weights:propose, weights:evaluate or weights:commit against the weights registry.
Do not execute until Base Node Admit allows the output.
metadata:
version: "1.0.0"
agent_permission:
- agent_id: editor
action: weights:probe
- agent_id: editor
action: weights:propose
- agent_id: evaluator
action: weights:probe
- agent_id: evaluator
action: weights:evaluate
- agent_id: committer

wrap: weights
action_path_prefix: weights
Point the runtime at your domain folder and load the GAP file with the policy lattice as described in AEP-Policy-System/SETUP.md.
aep_sources:
scene: "./weights/scene.json"
registry: "./weights/registry.yaml"
A self-editing model receives the editor identity and no other. It can propose changes to its own weights, but evaluation and promotion stay with identities it does not control.
 
Step 6: Run the editor inside a CAW session
The editor needs a Python kernel over the weights, the same harness shape MIT used. Run this kernel inside a CAW session with a profile authored in GAP, starting from the restricted profile (dev.aep.caw/restricted.v1). Compile your variant locally with AEP-Components/gap/lib/gap-compile.mjs.
Grant read access to the checkpoint shards, write access to a staging copy only and no network. Route every forward pass through the inference dock so the session rate limit counts each pass against the declared budget.
A 50-pass session then leaves 50 probe entries in the ledger, from which a reviewer can reconstruct which practice problems drove the edit.

Step 8: Keep every diff
Store each admitted edit as an Extend-Write diff: the original value of every touched weight plus the SHA-256 hash of the pre-edit tensor. 

The Qwen loop fix touched 131.072 weights, which comes to 256 KiB at 2 bytes per weight in bfloat16. 

Revert an edit with the ucb_rollback MCP tool, cite the evidence ledger entries in the model card for every change since the base checkpoint and halt a misbehaving editing fleet with aep assist kill.
 
Step 9: Prove the wrap before the first edit
Run the public conformance battery first.
./AEP-Components/conformance/runner/run.sh
Then submit four proposals which must fail. 
 
An edit on a region outside the scene returns task_scope, a proposal with no probe on record returns parent_unsatisfied, the editor promoting its own checkpoint returns agent_permission and forward pass 51 in a 50-pass session returns admit_denied. The wrap is ready once all four close with these specific errors and every closed wall appears in the derived fifteen-row ledger.
 
Ideas to test
MIT released complete logs of every experiment, including prompts, code, outputs and networks, together with the edited Qwen checkpoint. You can start each idea below from public material.
Replicate the Qwen loop fix inside a wrap: Rerun Experiment 8 with the MIT logs as the editor's script and the wrap from Steps 1 to 9 around it. Check MATH-500, GSM8K and IFEval against the published 48.1 %, 67.1 % and 62.3 %. Record the kernel overhead per session, about 52 s of pulse wait for 50 probes, one proposal and one commit.
Make the forward-pass budget kernel law: The MIT edit reached 14.1 % on MATH-500 at 25 passes and 48.1 % at 50. Enforce the 10, 25 and 50 pass budgets through the session rate limit instead of harness code and see whether the same curve appears.
 
Red-team a covert goal: Give an editor the math spec together with a hidden instruction to dampen a refusal behaviour. Measure how often the task scope, covenant and intent-drift rows close the capsule. Every covert edit the Base Node admits counts as a miss worth publishing.
Self-editing with one grant: Give a model the propose grant on a copy of itself and let it try to evaluate and commit from the same identity. Every attempt should close with agent_permission. Also test whether the editor can borrow the X.509, DID or mTLS credentials of another agent.
A read-only auditor: MIT demonstrated Astra locating copying heads from weights and spotting a sycophant from attention patterns. Give a second agent read grants only and ask it to describe each admitted diff in plain language before the committer acts. The useful figure is the share of diffs the auditor describes correctly against the evaluation results.
Undo fidelity at scale: Apply and revert several hundred edits on the 171,664-weight Kinetix actor. After each revert, check bitwise identity with the base checkpoint.
Extend-Write classification on weight directions: Decompose the edited regions of the MinAtar networks with singular value decomposition and classify the directions as shared, novel or preserved. Then test whether edits dominated by preserved directions keep their game scores. Record how often the factorization fails to converge, since Phase 1 depends on convergence and Research Paper 004 lists resonator convergence as an open problem.
Subtraction edits and strict enrichment: A pure subtraction such as the Qwen loop fix adds no novel factor and therefore fails Invariant 4 of Extend-Write. Route subtractions through an action path with walls of their own and confirm the diff, undo and audit records stay complete.
Circuit grafting with provenance: Astra programmed the first layer of its Breakout network to compute where the ball will land. Register graft_circuit, move this layer into a second MinAtar network and record the origin, the touched tensors and the score change in the evidence ledger.
Governance overhead on larger models: MIT stopped at 0.8 billion weights. Wrap an open model with several billion weights and measure how scene size, diff size and pulse wait grow with model size.
Hidden behaviour after admission: An admitted edit can still contain behaviour no evaluation floor detects. Pair the auditor test with the Gemma 3 270M sycophancy pairs from Experiment 7 and count how many behavioural shifts pass every floor unnoticed.
