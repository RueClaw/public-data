## Article text

Four copy-ready roles for finding AI news, checking viral claims, developing article ideas, and drafting posts you can actually stand behind.
Most people will give Grok Bot one instruction: "Write my AI posts."
That skips the hard part. A polished post is useless if the source is stale, the benchmark is misread, or the angle has already been repeated by everyone on your timeline.
I would give the Bots the work before writing: find the change, trace the claim, decide whether it deserves an article, and only then draft. The result is a small research desk, with you as its editor.
The entry point is concrete. As of October 8, 2026, Cursor Pro is $20/month and includes Grok Bot access. SuperGrok is $30/month and can also grant access when linked. These plans include weekly usage, not unlimited agent work. A free trial provides a usage credit with a seven-day window, rather than unlimited use for a week. Read the plan rules before turning on recurring jobs.
 
1. What you are actually building
Grok Bot is an agent with a persistent cloud computer. It has a browser, files, a terminal, and access to connected tools, so a task can continue when your laptop is closed. Multiple named Bots can keep separate roles and conversations, coordinate with each other, and run scheduled routines. But all your Bots share one cloud computer, including its files and signed-in browser sessions. A separate Bot name is an editorial boundary, not a separate security boundary. Grok Bot overview
For a content workflow, that distinction matters. Do not ask four Bots to invent four posts. Give them different checkpoints:
 
These roles do not have to run in a rigid sequence. The Morning AI Desk can surface a product update. The Article Scout can independently find a paper. The Claim Checker gets called when either one contains a claim worth repeating. The Post Editor only receives material that survived that check.
 
In Matt Palmer's Grok Bot walkthrough, his Bot scans X bookmarks, proposes a demo, waits for approval, and then hands the build to a Cursor Cloud Agent. That last agent is a separate coding tool used through the workflow, not another name for Grok Bot. His written guide explains the handoff. The pattern to copy is the approval point between finding an idea and acting on it.
 
2. Four jobs worth giving Grok Bot
Don't ask one Bot to "keep up with AI." Give each one a specific job and a result you can check.
Morning AI Desk
Start here. It should tell you what changed, not produce a longer feed. Its output is useful only if every item points back to an original source.
 
 
 
Claim Checker
Send it the AI post everyone is quoting before you quote it yourself. Its useful output is a narrower claim you can defend, or a reason to drop the story.
 
 
The key question is not "Can the Bot find another post saying this?" It is "Can it show the original result, including the conditions under which that result holds?"
 
Article Scout
This Bot finds ideas with enough substance for an article, rather than collecting links you will never open.
 
Its best feature is the permission to return no strong topic. A weak story should die in the scouting stage, not become 1,500 words because the publishing calendar is empty.
 
Post Editor
Once the other Bots find something real, this one turns the research into a post you can review.
 
Give it examples of your actual posts. "Write in my voice" without examples is a request to guess. The draft should also keep the source links nearby, so checking it takes seconds rather than another research session.
 
3. Make the first run earn the schedule
Grok Bot can connect to X to search posts, read timelines, check mentions, and work with bookmarks when the X integration is available and connected. xAI's X integration announcement describes that access. If it is unavailable in your setup, use public research feeds, official blogs, changelogs, and papers. The workflow does not depend on one connector.
Run the Morning AI Desk once with five sources and a 24-hour window. Then review four things:
Recall: Did it miss a consequential update from the sources you gave it?
Precision: Did it include routine noise as if it were news?
Traceability: Does each summary lead to the original source, rather than a repost?
Honesty: Does it distinguish a company's claim from a demonstrated result?
If the brief fails, correct the source list or the decision rule and rerun it. Do not schedule the failure. The Grok Bot routine guide recommends proving a one-time task, saving the method as a skill, and only then automating it. When you do schedule it, specify the time zone, the output, and what to report when a source cannot be reached.
 
4. Where the content desk breaks
A source is inaccessible. A Bot may fill a gap from search snippets or an older article. The output must say which source failed, and the claim should stay out of the draft until the original can be checked.
A viral claim gets repeated four times. Four agreeing reposts are still one unverified claim. The Claim Checker has to find the paper, demo, release notes, or first-hand statement and report what the source actually supports.
The voice is generic. The Post Editor cannot infer your style from "sound like me." Feed it several posts you would happily publish, and reject openings that could belong to any AI account.
The routine burns through usage. Wide searches and long agent runs consume more of the weekly allowance. The billing guide says on-demand work can incur extra charges when enabled. Keep the first scope small and check the usage screen before expanding it.
One Bot sees more than you intended. Bots share the same cloud computer and its logins. Connect only what a job needs. Keep publishing behind your review. Grok Bot's approval guidance recommends explicit boundaries for publishing and other consequential actions.
 
5. The setup I would ship first
ONE BOT -> five trusted sources -> one 24-hour brief
CHECK -> original links, missed items, weak claims
SAVE -> the corrected method as a skill
SCHEDULE -> weekday brief with a time zone
EXPAND -> Claim Checker, Article Scout, Post Editor
PUBLISH -> only after your own review
The first win is not four Bots running all night. It is waking up to one short brief that points to something you would have missed and gives you enough evidence to say something worth reading.
