## Text

If you've got $50,000 invested, a financial advisor would make $1.37 a day off you. That's less than a cup of coffee, and it's probably why none of them has ever called you when your stocks dropped.

The usual fee is about 1% a year, so fifty grand is worth $500 to an advisor. Run the same 1% on $5 million and it's $50,000, which buys a whole lot more of somebody's attention.

It shows up in who actually has one. About 27% of Americans use an advisor, and among millionaires it's 74%. Meanwhile 62% of us own stocks, so a huge chunk of the country is investing with nobody watching.

And what you're paying an advisor for, as far as I can tell, is mostly the part where they reach out first. One stock quietly grows into 10% of your account and you get a message asking if you want to sell some of it down. Most of us find that out by opening the app on a bad day.

Martin and his cofounder Akshat left Goldman Sachs to build that message for regular-sized accounts. Volaren connects to your brokerage and texts you why your portfolio moved, and if you like what it suggests you reply YES and it places the trade.

I went and checked the price, because that's usually where these things fall apart. It's $12 a month, with a free plan under it, and the price is the same on $5,000 as on $5 million. That makes sense when you think about it, since software doesn't get paid more for watching a bigger account.

I'd still be careful with any agent that has a buy button, and it looks like they get that trust is the hard part. Every account comes with $100,000 in practice money, so you can watch how it thinks for a while before it touches a real dollar.

It's early and it's in beta, so I can't tell you yet how good the suggestions are. But a text that tells you why your money moved is the part of having an advisor most people never got, and I'm glad somebody is building it at a price normal people can pay.
