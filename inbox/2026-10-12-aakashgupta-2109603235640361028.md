## Text

Coinbase took a free AI model, trained it on their own fraud data for a few weeks, and it now beats the most expensive model on the market at catching fraud. By a lot. Up to 35 points better on recall.

The free model has 9 billion parameters. It runs on a single GPU.

Here's why the big model never had a chance. Fraud patterns are proprietary. Coinbase's labeled fraud data has never touched the public internet, so no amount of pretraining compute buys you knowledge of it. Opus 4.5 walked into that benchmark having seen zero real Coinbase fraud. The little Qwen model had seen millions of examples.

Speed matters even more than accuracy here. The fine-tuned model answers in 0.68 seconds. Opus takes 1.5, and at the slow end it takes 3.3. Fraud checks run inside the checkout flow, so every one of those seconds is a customer staring at a spinner deciding whether to bail on the purchase.

And this playbook works anywhere. Insurance claims. Credit underwriting. Support tickets. Any company sitting on years of its own labeled data can train a small free model that beats the giant ones at that one job, for pennies on the API dollar.

The frontier model still earns its keep. It designs the system, writes the training pipeline, handles the weird stuff nothing prepared you for. But the millions of boring daily calls, the volume the AI labs were counting on as recurring revenue, quietly moves to a model they don't own and can't bill for.

Brian just handed every CFO in America the template.
