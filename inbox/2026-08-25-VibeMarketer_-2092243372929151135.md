## Article text

I am going to show you how to turn every correction, decision, and useful workflow your team gives AI into one company brain that makes everyone better.
Because right now, the smartest AI in your company probably works for one person.
One teammate has a Claude chat that understands the strategy. Another has taught Codex how you ship. Someone else has shown ChatGPT exactly how the founder wants proposals written.
Multiply that across every employee and agent. Your company is creating an incredible amount of intelligence, then trapping it inside private chats and personal setups.
A company brain changes that.
It turns what one person teaches AI into a better starting point for everyone.
Here is the build:
find the intelligence already hiding in your team's AI work;
give every agent the same map of the company;
turn corrections into shared learning; and
build the first version in HQ.
Throughout the article, I will give you working examples you can hand straight to your agent: the folder structure, map file, correction loop, and full build prompt.
Copy the blocks, paste them into your agent, and build along as you read.
1. Your company already has a brain. It is scattered everywhere.
The sales lead explains which objection kills the deal. The marketer corrects an old product claim. The operator shows an agent which numbers can be trusted. The founder rewrites the same sentence for the fifth time.
That is your company teaching AI how it thinks. Almost none of it escapes the chat.
So everyone gets faster, but each person ends up working with a slightly different version of the business.
One agent thinks an old campaign is current. Another follows a preference that was never approved. A third produces brilliant work because one employee happens to know the magic prompt.
The company is learning everywhere and remembering almost nowhere.
 
2. A company brain makes your best work reusable
Think of a company brain as everything your team has already figured out, made available to the next person and the next agent.
When someone begins a task, they should not have to reconstruct the company from scratch. They should be able to find:
what the company believes now;
which decisions replaced older decisions;
who owns the final call;
how this kind of work should be done;
which claims, sources, and examples are approved; and
what the team learned the last time it did this.
A new marketer starts with the positioning the founder spent months sharpening. The sales agent gets the objection handling that already works. Ops gets the workflow the team has already debugged.
Nobody needs the original chat or even needs to know who figured it out. The lesson belongs to the company now.
The value of a company brain is simple: learn once, improve everyone.
3. The brain should know how your company thinks, not only what it knows
Most company knowledge projects collect meeting notes, strategy decks, old campaigns, customer calls, product docs, and years of messages. Then they place a search box on top and call it a brain.
That gives AI more information. It does not give AI better judgment.
A useful company brain carries four things:
Memory: the facts, decisions, and history that matter now.
Judgment: the rules and standards that shape good work.
Capability: the skills and workers the company can run again.
Learning: the reviewed corrections that improve everything else.
Now connect them. One agent finds a trusted answer. Another inherits it. One worker solves a blocker. The next starts with the solution.
At that point, the brain stops behaving like a library and starts coordinating the company.
The brain becomes valuable when it can remember, decide, act, and share what worked.
 
4. Start with one workflow that matters
Start with the workflow your team is tired of explaining.
Maybe every content brief needs the same ten corrections. Maybe customer handoffs fall apart unless one specific person is involved. Maybe the Monday report takes two hours because nobody trusts the same numbers.
Pick one. The team should run it often, know what good looks like, and be able to review the result quickly.
Now ask:
What would an excellent employee need to know, believe, and do to complete this well?
For a content brief, that might mean current positioning, approved claims, audience pain points, voice rules, strong examples, and the final format.
Your first company brain can be this small:
company-brief.md: what we do and who we serve
priorities.md: what matters right now
decisions: what we decided and why
people: owners and approval boundaries
projects: current work and status
policies: rules that survive every chat
skills: proven ways to perform a task
workers: repeatable jobs an agent can run
learnings.md: corrections worth keeping
Paste that block into your agent and ask it to create the structure around your chosen workflow.
Leave anything you cannot maintain empty for now. One small brain that removes a painful weekly mistake is worth far more than a beautiful archive nobody uses.
5. Give every agent a map, then let the work pull the details
The first file every agent reads should be a tiny map of the company.
It does not need the entire business. It needs enough to understand where it is, what matters, and where to look next.
Use this as the starting point:
Company map

What we do

[Describe the company, customer, offer, and positioning in one short paragraph.]

What matters now

1. [Priority one]
2. [Priority two]
3. [Priority three]

Source order

1. [The system that owns current facts]
2. [The system that owns project status]
3. [The system that owns customer commitments]

Navigation

- decisions contains what we decided and why
- people contains owners and approval boundaries
- projects contains current work and status
- policies contains rules that apply every time
- skills contains proven ways to perform tasks
- workers contains repeatable jobs agents can run
- learnings.md contains reviewed corrections and lessons

Rules

- use the newest approved information
- when two sources conflict, follow the source order
- if the answer is missing, say what you could not find
- never turn a guess into company knowledge
This file is the front door. Paste the block into your agent, fill in the blanks together, and let each task decide which room it needs to enter.
A content agent pulls positioning, approved claims, and voice. A customer agent pulls account history and escalation rules. A coding agent pulls architecture decisions and security policy.
They all use the same brain without dragging the entire company into every conversation.
That matters because more context can create more confusion. Old campaigns compete with current positioning. Loose meeting notes compete with approved decisions. History starts looking like policy.
The fix is not more memory in every prompt. It is better navigation.
Give the agent the map first. Let it earn every deeper piece of context by needing it.
 
6. The correction is often more valuable than the output
Say a content agent writes a strong proposal but uses last quarter's positioning.
Most people fix the sentence, ship the proposal, and move on. The document gets better. Nothing else does.
The useful question is why the old positioning was still there.
Maybe the company brief is stale. Maybe nobody recorded the new decision. Maybe an old campaign is outranking the approved language.
Fix that layer, and the correction survives the proposal.
Here is where each fix should go:
Missing or outdated fact → company knowledge
New strategic choice → decision record
Repeated preference → policy
Proven technique → skill
Repeatable sequence → worker
Dangerous action → mechanical gate
Then review the change before sharing it with everyone.
The loop looks like this:
work → correction → route → review → share → better future work
Run that loop for long enough and every project leaves the company slightly smarter than it found it.
7. The brain needs one main home
Folders work brilliantly when one person owns the whole thing.
Then the second person joins. Someone edits a policy. Someone else uses the old version. A useful skill stays trapped on one laptop.
Add Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex, and one company brain quickly splits back into five personal ones. It needs one main home.
This is why we built HQ.
HQ keeps the company layer together while everyone keeps using the AI tool they prefer.
When one teammate finds a better way to do something, the change moves through review and into Main HQ. Once it is accepted, the rest of the team gets it too.
One teammate discovers a better way.
The improvement is reviewed.
Main HQ learns.
Every teammate starts further ahead.
One person figures something out on Tuesday. By Wednesday, the whole team can be working from it.
One person teaches the brain. The whole company remembers.
 
8. Paste this into your agent and build the first version now
Pick one workflow that matters, replace the brackets, and paste this into an agent with access to your company workspace.
Do not spend a month designing the perfect brain. Use this to create one useful loop, run it on real work, and improve it from the corrections.
Help me build the first version of our company brain around one recurring workflow.

Company

[Describe the company, customer, offer, and current priorities.]

Workflow

[Name one recurring workflow the team already performs.]

Outcome

[Describe what a strong completed result looks like.]

Your job

1. Identify the minimum company knowledge required to complete this workflow well.
2. List the decisions, policies, preferences, and approval boundaries that should guide the work.
3. Identify the people, projects, and systems that own the current truth.
4. Create a short company map that tells future agents where to find each type of context.
5. Design one repeatable worker with clear inputs, steps, output, and stopping conditions.
6. Create a correction-routing table for facts, decisions, policies, skills, workers, and safety gates.
7. Define the human review required before any improvement becomes shared company knowledge.

Return

- the minimum company-brain structure
- the source hierarchy
- the first policy
- the first worker specification
- the correction and review loop
- the missing information you need from me
- the exact first test we should run

Keep the first version small. Do not ingest the entire company. Build only what this workflow needs, and explain why every file or layer exists.
Run the first test. Every hesitation, guess, or stale answer shows you what the brain needs next.
You can build the first version in a folder. When you want the whole team to use it across Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex, bring it into HQ.
Start with one workflow in HQ. Add the context it needs, run it, fix what breaks, and share the improvement with everyone.
Your team is already teaching AI how the company works every day.
HQ makes sure the company gets to remember it.
