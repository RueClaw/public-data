## Article text

Many people aren't making the most of the new GPT-5.6, so I decided to write a very detailed guide that will turn it into the best tool in their lives.
The first thing everyone needs to understand: GPT-5.6 isn't one model, it's three: Sol, Terra, and Luna.
Many of you will find these names quite familiar - that is, if you have even the slightest interest in cryptocurrencies: Sol - Solana (still alive), but Terra and Luna are the two halves of the biggest wipeout in crypto history.
It's just a bit of fun, a kind of meme that has nothing to do with OpenAI, it's just that the name turned out to be pretty interesting.
Maybe Sam Altman invested in these cryptocurrencies, or maybe he didn't, but since they were quite popular, we can conclude that the idea for names came about precisely because of them.
Maybe there's also a pokemon connection - solgaleo and lunala are the legendary cover mascots of pokemon sun and moon (2016), literally the sun and moon pokemon. Sol, Luna, and even Terra fits the cosmic theme.
Anyway, back to the models.
The strangest launch in OpenAI history
June 26: OpenAI quietly previewed GPT-5.6 to ~20 trusted organizations.

Just think aboit it: the first frontier model in history released through a US government review process. OpenAI shared the model and its capabilities with the government before anyone else got to touch it.
And that's an understandable decision after what happened with Fable 5.
July 9: General availability.
GPT-5.6 rolled out across chatgpt, codex, work, and the api over roughly 24 hours globally.

Same day, the standalone codex app got folded into the new ChatGPT desktop app - one surface for chat and coding instead of two.
 
But in any case, the model was first handed over to the government, where some tests were conducted, as a result of those tests, the model was modified - whether for the better or for the worse, only time will tell (just a guess), BUT it is almost 100% certain that some restrictions were imposed on it.
And that’s understandable, without that, access to models would simply have been blocked.
The naming philosophy
Buried deep in the announcement is the single most important line of the entire release:
The number identifies a model’s generation, while Sol, Terra, and Luna identify durable capability tiers that can advance on their own cadence.
OpenAI just quietly decoupled "how smart" from "how new".
For years their naming was a mess: GPT-4o, o1, o3-mini-high, GPT-5.4-pro.. Constant confusion, broken pipelines every quarter.
Now they’ve fixed it:
The number (5.6) = the generation.
Sol / Terra / Luna = independent capability tiers that can evolve separately.
Even Sam Altman highlighted exactly this benefit:
 
This isn’t just rebranding, It’s a fundamental shift in how frontier labs will release models going forward.
The new system:
Sol = the sun = flagship + deep reasoning + most expensive.
Terra = the earth  = the balanced daily workhorse.
Luna = the moon  = fastest + cheapest + high volume.
What this means in practice:
A future GPT-5.7 Luna can ship without touching Sol. 
A Sol upgrade doesn't force your Terra pipelines to re-test. 

if you build an agent on Terra today, the contract is "balanced cost and capability" - and that contract survives the next three generation bumps.
This mirrors where the whole industry converged:
Anthropic has Haiku/Sonnet/Opus, Google has Flash/Pro. 

OpenAI was the last holdout, and their version is arguably the cleanest - celestial bodies, instantly readable: sun is strongest, moon is lightest, earth is where you live.

Sol is the model you call in for the hard case. It sets a new state of the art on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (command-line workflows requiring planning, iteration, and tool coordination) and shifts the performance-efficiency frontier on long-horizon security work.
The sleeper stat:

On OSWorld 2.0 (computer use), Sol hits 62.6% using 85% fewer output tokens than Claude Opus 4.8. that token efficiency shows up everywhere in this release.
 
Еhere's also Sol Pro - an even heavier variant exclusive to Pro and Enterprise plans, for the absolute hardest tasks.
2. GPT-5.6 Terra - the quiet hero
[ WHAT IT IS ]: the balanced everyday model.
[ PRICE ]: $2.50 input / $15 output per 1M tokens.
[ BUILT FOR ]: 90% of normal work: writing, analysis, planning, standard coding.
[ THE KICKER ]: competitive with GPT-5.5 at HALF the cost.
Terra is the tier most work should land on. It performs at roughly the level of GPT-5.5, but for 2x less money.
And the important part: Free users get Terra
This is the first time a brand-new frontier generation reached free accounts on launch week. OpenAI is giving away flagship-level intelligence to a billion people, because the real game is getting everyone hooked on Work and agentic workflows.
3. GPT-5.6 Luna - the volume machine
[ WHAT IT IS ]: fastest, cheapest tier.
[ PRICE ]: $1 input / $6 output per 1M tokens.
[ BUILT FOR ]: classification, extraction, formatting, short drafts, routing, high-volume pipelines.
Luna is where volume-scale deployment lives. If your task is repetitive, easy to validate, or one step in a bigger pipeline - this is your model. Paying Sol prices for Luna tasks is the single most common money leak in AI usage right now.

Effort levels
GPT-5.6 doesn't just give you three models. each model has a reasoning dial - and the top settings are where the release gets wild.
Effort ladder:

Low: instant answers, minimal reasoning.
Medium: the default balance.
High: extended thinking for harder problems.
Max: maximum single-agent reasoning depth.
Ultra: beyond a single agent entirely
Max is simple: give Sol the most time to think deeply, use it for genuinely hard problems where the answer matters more than the wait.
Ultra is the headline feature of the entire release:
Ultra mode doesn't just think longer, it spins up 4 sub-agents working in parallel (configurable up to 16 through the API), delegates sub-tasks to them, and coordinates their results into one answer.
Ultra mode:
Single Sol: 88.8% Terminal-Bench & ~$1.70 per run.
Sol ultra: 91.9% Terminal-Bench & ~$5.00 per run (4 agents parallel, 3x cost, 3.1 points).
Think about what this means: multi-agent orchestration - the thing people spent all of 2026 building with frameworks, worktrees, and duct tape - is now a toggle in a consumer product.
Ultra runs in ChatGPT Work for Pro/Enterprise, and in Codex for Plus and above. In the API, the multi-agent beta in the Responses API builds ultra-like flows.
This effort level needs for a critical long-horizon run where failure costs more than $5.
Pricing & Cahing trick
 
For context against the competition: Claude Fable 5 costs 2x what Sol costs per token, and 4x what Terra costs.
But the real money is in caching: GPT-5.6 introduces predictable prompt caching:
Cache WRITES: 1.25x the uncached input rate.
Cache READS: 90% DISCOUNT on input.
Cache life: 30-minute minimum TTL.
NEW: explicit cache breakpoints, you control exactly where the cache cuts.
What this means in practice: if you run an agent that re-sends a big stable prefix every turn (system prompt, tool definitions, project context - which is every coding agent ever), you pay 1.25x once to write the cache, then 0.1x on every subsequent read.
The math on a real workload:
[ SCENARIO ]: agent with 50K-token stable prefix, 40 turns/day on Sol.
WITHOUT caching:
50K × 40 turns × $5/1M = $10.00/day on prefix alone
WITH caching:
Write once:  50K × $6.25/1M = $0.31
39 reads: 50K × 39 × $0.50/1M = $0.98

Total: $1.29/day - an 87% cut
if you're running agents and not using cache breakpoints, you are donating money to OpenAI. Same lesson as my GLM guide: the token price lies. cost per task is the real metric.

The most important rule:
Sort Models & Efforts by risk, not by hype:

High consequence, high ambiguity, multi-step tool use -> start higher.
Repetitive, easy to validate, part of a pipeline -> start lower.
The obvious mistake is reading "flagship" and turning every task into a flagship task. that buys you slower workflows and 5x cost for results you can't tell apart.
Chat vs Work vs Codex
GPT-5.6 lands across three product surfaces, and the quota system between them catches everyone off guard:
Chat -> answers questions ->  its OWN separate quota.
Work -> does your work -> shares "agentic usage" pool.
Codex -> writes your code -> shares "agentic usage" pool.
Chat all day - your agent quota is untouched, but Work and Codex eat from the same plate.
One more structural change: as of July 9, the standalone Codex app is merging into the new ChatGPT desktop app. One app, three modes - Chat, Work, Codex. developers can set Codex as the default view.
The honest part
GPT-5.6 is not a clean sweep, and pretending otherwise helps nobody.
Where it genuinely leads: 
Agentic work, Agents' Last Exam, Terminal-Bench, computer use, token efficiency - the numbers are real and the gaps are big. If your workload is long-horizon agents, tools, and terminals, this is the best family on the market today, at prices Anthropic can't currently touch.
Where it doesn't: 
Deep software engineering: the 15-point SWE-Bench Pro gap vs Fable 5 is real, and OpenAI's "the benchmark is broken" response is doing a lot of heavy lifting. For hard single-repo coding, Claude keeps the crown for now.
What to watch: 
Whether the "durable tier" promise holds. The whole Sol/Terra/Luna philosophy only pays off if OpenAI actually upgrades tiers independently instead of just shipping GPT-5.7 and starting over.
The actual takeaway: 
The era of "one best model" is over - killed by the very company that started it. Every provider now ships a spectrum, and the scarce skill isn't access anymore. It's routing: knowing which tasks deserve Sol, which run fine on Terra, and which go to Luna.
